16 experiments across 6 hyperparameter axes · Metric: policy agreement % vs exact minimax baseline · Random baseline = 25%
What we're measuring: How closely does DQN's greedy policy match the exact minimax solution computed by value iteration? 25% = random (4 actions). Higher = better approximation of the optimal policy. All experiments: 4,000 episodes, γ=0.9, pure ±1 reward, deterministic A10 transitions.

Key findings:
• Slow epsilon decay wins (54.3%) — more exploration time lets DQN discover more of the state space before committing.
• Self-play opponent (50.4%) outperforms minimax opponent (44.3%) — playing against itself creates diverse training situations.
• LR=5e-4 is the sweet spot — 1e-2 overshoots (38%), 1e-4 underfits (45.5%).
• All configs significantly beat random (25%) but fall short of exact (100%) — this is the expected DQN vs tabular gap.
• Network size and depth matter less than exploration strategy.
